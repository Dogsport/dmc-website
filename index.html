DMC Homepage Redesign · Final A/B Review

Same club. Same content. Two genuinely different experiences.

Both concepts preserve the DMC’s real content and navigation, but they solve the homepage differently. The comparison is intentionally about information hierarchy, task completion, visual identity and mobile usability—not simply light versus dark colors.

1. Make sense of the information

Inspired by Abby Covert: the page tells a clear sequence—who DMC is, what is happening now, where users can go, and why the club matters.

2. Reduce cognitive load

Inspired by John Maeda and Don Norman: fewer competing messages, visible signifiers, obvious actions, readable labels and progressive disclosure.

3. Design with real content

Inspired by Erika Hall and content-strategy practice: real DMC/Facebook copy and real imagery are used instead of idealized placeholder content.

Editorial Light
Concept A

Editorial Light

A modern association experience: bright but not washed out, image-led, structured, calm and highly scannable.

  • Full-width hero with minimal text
  • Four task-based quick-access modules below the hero
  • Facebook shown as a recognizable, natural-ratio social feed
  • History and breeding presented as editorial storytelling
  • Partner area uses an ordered logo wall rather than floating cards

Performance Dark

A more emotional, sport-led experience: cinematic hero, stronger contrast, segmented action bar and editorial Facebook stream.

  • Full-width cinematic hero with stronger DMC identity
  • RSV-inspired task rail, redesigned into a DMC-specific command bar
  • Facebook posts become image/content story rows—not card masonry
  • 1891 history section uses a distinctive three-part composition
  • Partners receive a bold red closing section

How to A/B test the two concepts

Do not ask only “Which one looks nicer?”. Give both versions the same practical tasks and record completion time, hesitation and confidence.

Task 1Find the next DMC event.
Task 2Find current breeding / litter information.
Task 3Find the latest important Facebook update.
Task 4Find how to become a member.
Decision rule: prefer the concept that users understand faster and complete these tasks with less hesitation. Visual preference is the second criterion, not the first.
